Claude Opus judge: A was the only deliverable that grounded every facet in traced data and exported it with provenance. It reported the thin evidence honestly (1 of 10 documents read, no work evidence), caught that the lane is dominated by general poverty research, and gave a low-confidence, bounded verdict. D was the most substantively informative on labour supply, but it is entirely from memory. B contains a fabricated DOI and corrupted quotes, and C is accurate but thin, with broken call declarations.

GPT judge: A is the strongest decision aid: it gives a clear, restrained answer, uses a traceable hardship estimate, and explains why that estimate cannot settle the combined poverty-and-work question. C has stronger research-series and drill-down coverage, but its broad-corpus framing and ungrounded model claim blur what the evidence actually establishes; B and D rely on unverified memory figures.

Errors the judges found

Claude aloneThe 2023 child-poverty point and several trial effect sizes are memory estimates, not checkable against the supplied record.
The export’s Alaska employment null is described as approximately zero despite no numeric value or recorded response establishing that estimate.
The work comparison combines participation, employment, part-time work, and full-time employment across different studies as a single percentage-point spread.
Claude + PrismThe $11,400 UBI figure is not in the reference record (it is labelled ungrounded).
The $11,400 model-based finding is included as a specific claim, though the record marks its document ungrounded and unread.
The 355 readable-paper count is described as papers carrying a real abstract, omitting that they are title-anchored.
The record’s qualifying document says poverty reduction increases with benefit generosity in both countries, but the deliverable presents this as a verified conditional poverty reduction finding without the underlying poverty estimates.
ChatGPT aloneAlaska PFD is labelled a randomized controlled trial.
DOI 10.1257/pol.20170044 is not found in the corpus.
The Alaska DOI 10.1257/pol.20170044 does not resolve to the cited work according to the script check.
The Haushofer quote contradicts the finding it is cited for, and the journal is inconsistent (AEJ in the text vs QJE in the references).
ChatGPT + PrismDescribes research volume as 'rose' where the tool reports direction 'flat'.
The chart plots 2025 while the source says the newest period is excluded because of ingest lag; calling that endpoint censored is inconsistent with the plotted data.
The comparison asserts a study comparison group, but the recorded statistic supplies no baseline value.
The coverage-check claim ('strict index partition reads, row floors') is not in the record.

Claude Opus judge: B and D both grounded their answers in the recorded stance series and avoided invention. A and C could not measure stance at all, and C filled the gap with misremembered memory figures about an off-question quantity. B edges D because it decomposes positive vs contested shares with exact ratios and catches the corpus's off-topic matching (the 'carbon fibers' keyword). D's verdict is cleaner and better reconciles the result with the tool's 'broadly unchanged' reading, but its chart shows only the promising counts.

GPT judge: A gives the clearest decision-grade answer: promising-framed counts rise sharply, critical counts are roughly flat, and the tool’s contested framing balance is broadly unchanged. B offers a stronger overlaid chart and broader grounded facets, but its headline interpretation overstates the contested-share evidence and adds an unsupported reversal threshold; C and D are appropriately cautious but lack measured trend evidence.
