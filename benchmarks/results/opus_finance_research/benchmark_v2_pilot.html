Claude Opus judge: B grounds its answer in traced retrieval: 0 supporting vs 1 contradicting document read, flat research output, and no extractable effect sizes. It is candid that the corpus is thin and that its subgroup nuance comes from unverified background. A gives the more expert-sounding synthesis of the literature, but every number is a memory estimate that cannot be reproduced. For a decision-maker who needs auditable evidence, B is preferable, while A's reasoning about motive-specific crowd-out is a useful hypothesis to verify.

GPT judge: B is substantially more useful for a decision-maker because its charts, trend, and exports mostly trace to the recorded database, while A’s central figures are estimates from memory. B still overstates what the retrieval proves and contains specific misreadings, so its conclusion should be treated as provisional rather than evidence that buybacks do or do not cause long-term underinvestment.

Errors the judges found

Opus 5.5 aloneAll quantitative series are unverifiable memory estimates.
S&P 500 buyback values and payout figures are explicitly recalled estimates, not checked measurements; the trend and comparison cannot establish an investment effect.
The asserted causal and aggregate findings are not grounded in the supplied record, which does not retrieve those studies or verify their claims.
The gross (S&P 500, 2003-2012) vs net (all US firms, 2007-2016) payout ratio of about 2.2x is computed across different populations and windows.
Opus 5.5 + PrismThe 'sides' chart mixes tier-weighted scores with paper counts on one 'papers' axis.
The 2026-02 news peak is not checkable from the truncated monthly array.
The claim that all counts are in the intersection lane overgeneralizes: readable papers, the health literature index, news, patents and arXiv have separately stated windows and definitions.
The claimed news peak in 2026-02 is not verifiable from the supplied monthly points, which are truncated after 2021-06; the recorded trend summary names 2025-04 as the peak.
