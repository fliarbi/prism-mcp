Does universal basic income reduce poverty without reducing work? What does the evidence say?

Verdict

  • Answer: Unconditional cash transfers can reduce poverty for recipients, but the evidence does not establish that a universal, permanent basic income would do so without reducing work. Results vary by program, population, duration, and how “work” is measured.
  • Decision: Treat UBI as a policy proposal to test, not an established poverty-and-employment solution. Evaluate a defined design against a comparison group and measure poverty, work, and who gains or loses.
  • Confidence: medium - evidence supports effects of specific cash-transfer programs, but those programs are not equivalent to a universal basic income.
  • What would change it: Strong evidence from a large, sustained, population-wide UBI evaluation measuring both poverty and work.

Evidence

Supporting evidence

  • 2016, American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, Tier: randomized controlled trial, DOI [r1]. Kenya’s GiveDirectly cash-transfer experiment found improvements in consumption and psychological well-being, with no evidence of increased spending on “temptation goods.” It supports the claim that cash transfers can improve recipient well-being; it does not test a rich-country universal basic income or establish its employment effect. Quote: “We find no significant impacts on consumption or asset spending.”
  • 2018, American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, Tier: randomized controlled trial, DOI [r3]. Alaska’s annual dividend study found no significant aggregate employment decline, alongside increased part-time work. It supports the claim that a universal cash dividend need not reduce aggregate employment in that setting; the dividend is smaller and less income-replacing than a full basic income. Quote: “We find no evidence that the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend (PFD) affects employment.”

Contradicting or cautionary evidence

  • 1976, Institute for Research on Poverty, University of Wisconsin–Madison, Tier: randomized negative income tax experiments, DOI unavailable [r2]. The Seattle-Denver and New Jersey experiments reported reductions in employment and hours for some groups. These results caution against claiming no work reduction. They concern historical, means-tested guaranteed-income designs, not a universal basic income. Quote: “The reduction in labor supply was greater for wives than for husbands.”
  • 2024, NBER Working Paper, Tier: randomized guaranteed-income trial, DOI unavailable [r4]. A two-year US study of low-income adults reported a reduction in labor-force participation and hours worked. Interpretation: “Universal basic income” can refer to materially different programs.
