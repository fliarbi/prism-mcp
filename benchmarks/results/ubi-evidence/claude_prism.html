Does universal basic income reduce poverty without reducing work? What does the evidence say?

Verdict

  • Answer: The Prism evidence set cannot confirm that UBI reduces poverty without reducing work. Verified documents show only conditional poverty reductions that rise with benefit generosity [r1] and reduced hardship from a one-time cash transfer [c3]. No retrieved document tests work effects directly.
  • Decision: Treat the claim as plausible but unproven. Fund or design a pilot with pre-registered poverty and employment outcomes rather than relying on this evidence set. Pull the pilot and RCT literature (e.g. the OpenResearch and Finnish trials) before committing.
  • Confidence: low - of 10 documents read, 1 qualifies the claim, 8 are unrelated and 1 is unread [c1]; none addresses employment.
  • What would change it: Verified results from multi-year, universal-design trials that report both poverty and hours worked.

What the data shows

  • Volumes (lane: intersection): 36,874 typed research papers, 2017-2025 [c1]. Only 355 carry a real abstract, 1990-2025 [c1]. the health literature index, patents and arXiv each show 0 [c1]. News shows 6 articles, 2019-01 to 2026-06 [c1].
  • Stance labels over the 36,874 papers: supporting 7,229, qualifying 11,823, contradicting 3,310, neutral 14,512; contested share 41.0% [c1]. These label each paper's framing, not a verdict on the claim [c1]. The lane is broad: the topic "Income, Poverty, and Inequality" alone has 36,729 papers [c1], so these counts mostly reflect general poverty research, not UBI. Only 661 papers have "universal basic income" in the title [c1].
  • Tally of the 10 documents checked: supports 0, contradicts 0, qualifies 1, unrelated 8, unread 1 [c1]. Tier-weighted: qualifies 3, others 0 [c1]. Read 1, ungrounded 1 [c1]. The counter-evidence call returned no supporting or contradicting documents [c2].
  • Effect size: material hardship fell by 0.17 standard deviations among families with under $500 of earnings in the prior month, roughly the bottom half of monthly earnings in the sample [c3]. This was a one-time unconditional transfer to SNAP-receiving families in 12 states, from pre-specified exploratory analyses [c3]. It is not universal, not recurring, and not a work outcome.
  • Qualifying finding: poverty reduction increases with benefit generosity in both countries studied, in normal times and in crisis [c1].
  • Model-based, unread: an $11,400 UBI replacing non-medical programs and unemployment benefits improves welfare, especially for less-educated people [c1]. The tool marked this unverified and ungrounded, so treat it as a claim only.
  • Work effects: not measurable from this data. No retrieved document reports employment or hours.

Evidence

Tier labels are Prism's.

  • Qualifies: Nichelatti, Jouste, Rattenhuber, 2025, book chapter, tier article [r1]. Quote: "In normal times and in times of crisis, poverty reduction increases with the generosity of the UBI benefit amount in both countries."
  • Related effect size: COVID Cash Transfer Study, 2023, J. Policy Analysis and Management, tier article [r2]. Quote: "we find significant reductions in material hardship (‐0.17 standard deviations) among families with less than $500 of earnings in the previous month."
  • Unread (ungrounded): Santos and Rauh, 2025, no DOI, id prism:W7114821397 [r3]. Claimed: "Replacing non-medical programs and unemployment benefits with an $11,400 UBI improves welfare further..."
  • Judged unrelated to poverty: Hanna & Olken 2018 on UBI vs targeted transfers [r4]; Gentilini et al. 2019 World Bank guide [r5]; Wilson & McDaid 2021 on mental health [r6]; Pega et al. 2022 Cochrane on health service use [r7]; Baird et al. 2013 on schooling [r8].
  • Contradicting documents: none found [c2].

Method and limits

  • Windows: research 2017-2025 (ends 2025-12-31, about 10 months before today); news to 2026-06-01 (about 5 months before today) [c1]. Newest periods are censored because ingest lags [c3].
  • Lane: intersection (subject AND outcome) [c1]. Match is by topic labels and title phrases, so it is broad.
  • Counts are paper counts and framing labels, not studies of UBI or verdicts. Only 10 documents were examined, 1 read in full [c1].
  • Not measurable here: effects on work, long-run effects, and results from the major pilot and RCT literature, which the tool did not surface. Only one effect size was found [c3]. The ratios and shares in Comparison are my arithmetic on tool counts (est.).
  • Interpretation: the low-confidence verdict and the decision advice are mine.
