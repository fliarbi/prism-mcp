Does universal basic income reduce poverty without reducing work? What does the evidence say?

Verdict

  • Answer: Cash transfers can reduce material hardship, but the supplied evidence does not establish that a permanent universal basic income (UBI) reduces poverty while preserving work. The one quantified result is for a one-time transfer to a subgroup of families receiving SNAP, not a universal, ongoing UBI [c3][r1].
  • Decision: Treat UBI’s combined poverty-and-work effect as unproven. Use the available evidence to motivate evaluation of a specific benefit design, with poverty and employment outcomes measured separately.
  • Confidence: medium - the supplied evidence includes a verified hardship estimate, but no verified estimate of UBI’s effect on poverty rates or work.
  • What would change it: Credible evaluations of ongoing universal payments measuring both poverty and employment over time.

What the data shows

Prism’s research index reports 36,874 papers in its intersection lane for UBI and poverty topics [c1]. Its stance labels classify 7,229 as supporting, 11,823 as qualifying, 3,310 as contradicting, and 14,512 as neutral; these are corpus labels, not verified findings on the question [c1]. Of the selected documents, 1 was read and 1 was ungrounded; the tier-weighted tally records 3 qualifying points and none supporting or contradicting [c1]. A separate counter-evidence call found no verified supporting or contradicting document verdicts among its selected records [c2].

The measurable effect is a reduction of 0.17 standard deviations in material hardship among families with less than $500 in prior-month earnings, described as roughly the bottom 50% of that study sample [c3]. This was a pre-specified exploratory analysis of a one-time unconditional transfer to families receiving SNAP; it is not an estimate of poverty-rate or employment effects of permanent UBI [c3][r1]. The available calls cannot measure whether UBI reduces poverty without reducing work.

Method and limits

Windows end in 2025 for indexed research, 2026-06-01 for news, and 2024 for patents; relative to 2026-10-23, these endpoints are about 10 months, 4 months, and 22 months old, respectively [c1][c3]. Lanes are Prism’s “intersection” of subject and outcome terms, not a curated set of UBI trials [c1]. Coverage checks passed strict index partition reads and historical row floors, which Prism says are not proof of complete ingestion [c1][c3]. The research series excludes the newest period because publication ingest lags; news also excludes its newest period for that reason [c3]. Match mode includes title/topic phrase matching, and the research call notes that some topic phrases are broad [c1]. Counts are index volumes and stance labels, not causal effect estimates. The −0.17 standard-deviation result is a study-reported exploratory subgroup estimate; interpreting it as evidence about permanent universal UBI or employment would exceed what the data supplied can answer [c3][r1].
