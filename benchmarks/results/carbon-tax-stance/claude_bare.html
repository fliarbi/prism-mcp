Claude alone
Is research on carbon taxes becoming more favourable or more critical?

Verdict

  • Answer: From memory alone, I cannot measure a yearly orientation series. The qualitative literature suggests research has moved from broadly favourable (efficiency, emissions cuts) toward a more nuanced mix: still supportive on effectiveness, more critical on distribution, political acceptability and adequacy of price levels. This is interpretation (est.), not a measured count.
  • Decision: Treat carbon pricing as effective but design-dependent. Fund revenue recycling and acceptability work. Do not cite a "favourable vs critical" ratio until the Prism calls below are run.
  • Confidence: low - no Prism calls were run, and every figure here is a memory-based estimate.
  • What would change it: A yearly stance series from research.stance_trends showing a sustained rise or fall in critical share.

What the data shows

No Prism call was executed, so there are no [cN] values. Everything numeric below is labelled (est.) or comes from a named reference.

  • Carbon pricing covers roughly 24% of global emissions (est.) according to World Bank reporting [r1].
  • Ex-post studies of the Swedish and British carbon pricing find emission reductions of a few percent to about 6% (est.) [r2].
  • A synthesis of ex-post evaluations found a median reduction of around 5–21% (est.) [r3]. I am unsure of the exact range.
  • British Columbia studies reported emission reductions of about 5–15% (est.) with small economic effects [r4].
  • Yellow-vest protests in France, from late 2018 (est.), are the usual example of the acceptability problem [r5].

Not answerable from memory: the number of papers per year, the share classed favourable vs critical, and any ratio of the two.

Method and limits

  • Windows: the chart covers 2010-01-01 to 2024-12-31, which is a gap of about 22 months to today (2026-10-23). The memory cutoff is June 2026.
  • Lanes: only the research lane was considered, from memory. No news lane was used.
  • Coverage: a handful of well-known papers, which is not a systematic sample.
  • Censoring: recent years are underrepresented in my memory.
  • Match mode: not applicable because no search was run. Phrase expansion to "carbon pricing" would change any counts, and thin years would need flagging.
  • The counts are rough policy-adoption estimates and are not paper counts.
  • Interpretation: the whole "more nuanced" verdict is my reading, not a measurement.
  • Cannot answer: the yearly orientation series, the early-vs-late framing ratio, or which years are thin.
