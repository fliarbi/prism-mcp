Is research on carbon taxes becoming more favourable or more critical?

Verdict

  • Answer: Research in this corpus is becoming more favourable in its promising framing, while critical framing is roughly flat; the balance of contested framing has changed little [c1]. This is a shift in annual counts, not proof that researchers’ underlying judgments have changed.
  • Decision: Treat the trend as a reason to examine the newer positive findings, while retaining scrutiny of limitations and counterevidence.
  • Confidence: medium - the time series is consistent, but orientation labels describe papers’ framing and the search combines related phrases [c1].
  • What would change it: A paper level comparison using a stable, manually checked sample and a separate critical paper series.

What the data shows

The research stance series covers 2017–2025 [c1] and includes 2,775 papers [c1]. Annual promising papers rose from 33 in 2017 to 214 in 2025 [c1]. Critical papers were 16 in 2017 and 15 in 2025, with a peak of 28 in 2020 [c1]. The combined positive share was 12.6% in 2017 and 24.4% in 2025; contested share was 25.0% and 16.9%, respectively [c1].

The tool’s early versus late comparison says contested framing moved from 22.1% to 19.9%, a decline of 2.2 percentage points, and characterizes the balance as broadly unchanged [c1]. These series are counts and framing shares, not a direct measure of whether carbon taxes work.

Comparison

Using the first and last years as the baseline, promising counts rose from 33 to 214 papers, while critical counts moved from 16 to 15 [c1]. Promising papers were 2.1 times critical papers in 2017 and 14.3 times critical papers in 2025 [c1]. These are comparisons of annual counts; they do not establish that the share of all research with those views changed by the same ratio.

Method and limits

The indexed window is 2017–2025 [c1][c2]. Its final date is 2025-12-31 [c1][c2], about 9 months and 23 days before today, 2026-10-23 [c1]. The data is by calendar year; the supplied results do not give publication dates within the year for the full trend series.

The lane is typed research indexed against the combined phrase set [c1]. The trend call reports no thin years [c1]. Coverage checks passed the tool’s index partition reads and historical row floors, but the tool explicitly says this is not proof of complete ingestion [c1][c2]. The browse call matches phrases in titles and paper keywords; it does not show that every result substantively studies carbon taxes [c2]. Its returned examples are sorted recent and limited, so they are illustrations, not a representative sample [c2].

Counts show how many indexed papers received each framing label. Shares describe the tool’s positive and contested groupings; orientation is how a paper frames its finding, not a verdict on truth [c1]. The interpretation that research is becoming more favourable rests on the rise in promising counts, while the critical series and contested-share comparison qualify that reading [c1]. The supplied data cannot establish causal impact, research quality, author agreement, or the arguments in specific critical papers.
