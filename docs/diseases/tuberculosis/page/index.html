Nine ways to explore the research, run on this disease

Each method below is one or two MCP calls. The card gives the question it answers, the call exactly as made, what came back, a reading of that result and its limits.

Chaining them. Start with the facets and history views to see the size and composition of the literature, which is large, mostly empirical and growing, from 10,943 papers in 2017 to 15,197 in 2025. Then use the screening table to find which risk factors carry quoted effect sizes, and note that HIV, malnutrition and air pollution returned none. Follow with gap mapping on the factors that did report figures, such as diabetes, to check which sub-components are covered. For any specific claim, run triangulation and head-to-head comparisons, and treat unrelated or unread document verdicts as a prompt to retrieve better sources, as happened with isoniazid preventive therapy and Xpert. Finally, use the frontier scan, framing drift and geography views to place findings in context: recent high-citation work, steady framing, and where studies are conducted compared with where they are reported.

Reading. The literature is mainly descriptive and promising in orientation: descriptive 28,091 papers (36.0%), promising 20,620 (26.5%), problem_identifying 13,324 (17.1%), breakthrough 6,704 (8.6%), cautionary 4,621 (5.9%), comparative 3,875 (5.0%) and critical 721 (0.9%). By contribution, empirical work dominates at 59,057, followed by review (9,292), methodological (6,654), position (2,818) and theoretical (135). The topic list is led by Tuberculosis Research and Epidemiology (38,091), then Mycobacterium research and diagnosis (14,789) and Diagnosis and treatment of tuberculosis (11,785). Some topics such as COVID-19 diagnosis using AI (1,840) appear because of keyword overlap, and are not core tuberculosis work. The most-cited-since-2020 list holds only 2025 papers with 0 or 1 citations, so it reflects recency and not influence, and it should not be used to judge what matters. A careful researcher would filter by contribution type and sort for older, well-cited work instead, and would check whether the small critical share reflects the field or the labelling.

The year in the news

From mid‑2025 the field moved from a broad surge of research on neuro‑TB, novel targets and pediatric diagnostics toward more applied trials and public awareness campaigns, especially in high‑burden countries. By late 2025 the focus shifted to policy reforms and large‑scale vaccination safety studies, while India reported a notable decline in incidence. Early 2026 highlighted biological breakthroughs and cost‑effective treatment regimens, but also underscored persistent gaps: drug‑resistant transmission, under‑diagnosis in Europe, and emerging funding and vaccine‑hesitancy challenges. The coming months will test whether new AI‑driven diagnostics, high‑dose rifampicin strategies, and international financing can sustain momentum against a disease that remains a global public‑health threat.

2025-06 · 716 articles · turning point
June 2025 saw a surge of TB research spanning neuroimaging, novel therapeutic targets, pediatric diagnostics, transplant risks, and promising regional eradication efforts.
• A cohort study detailed the diverse MRI presentations of central nervous system tuberculosis, highlighting the complexity of neuro‑TB diagnosis.
• Research identified M. tuberculosis adhesins as potential intervention points and introduced low‑complexity manual nucleic acid amplification tests for rapid pediatric pulmonary TB diagnosis, indicating advances in both treatment and detection.
• Analyses of TB after renal transplantation and the immunogenicity of a Mycobacterium intracellulare isolate revealed heightened vulnerability in transplant recipients and the prospect of cross‑protective immunity for vaccine development.
• Expert insights underscored tangible progress toward eradication, citing successful prevention strategies in Nariño, Colombia, as a model for broader TB‑free aspirations.
2025-07 · 847 articles · escalating
July 2025 saw new strides in TB detection, treatment trials, and heightened public awareness across India, South Africa, China, and media.
• A study in western Rajasthan, India, highlighted the need to bridge the diagnostic gap by implementing early detection of pulmonary TB among people living with HIV, aiming to reduce delayed diagnoses and improve outcomes.
• Researchers in South Africa’s Limpopo province reported in‑vitro antioxidant and antimycobacterial activity of locally used medicinal plants, suggesting alternative options against drug‑resistant Mycobacterium tuberculosis strains.
• A safety and feasibility trial in Chinese children showed that a one‑month daily rifapentine plus isoniazid regimen achieved adequate plasma concentrations, offering a shorter preventive therapy for tuberculosis infection.
• The TV series “Goodbye Deutschland” featured a woman dying of TB in Bali, sparking public shock and raising awareness of tuberculosis as a deadly infectious disease worldwide.
2025-08 · 844 articles · escalating
August 2025 saw new insights into pediatric TB treatment adherence, promising therapeutic and diagnostic advances, and strategies to curb drug‑resistant TB
• Research identified key predictors of early discontinuation of preventive therapy among children exposed to multidrug‑resistant TB, highlighting adherence challenges for WHO‑recommended levofloxacin regimens.
• Two novel agents showed dual promise: netupitant demonstrated potent activity against persister MTB cells that drive relapse, and a solvatochromic naphthalimide‑benzothiazinone probe combined rapid detection with bacterial inhibition.
• Long‑read amplicon sequencing was highlighted as a rapid, cost‑effective, user‑friendly diagnostic platform for TB and drug‑resistant TB, aligning with WHO’s call for better detection tools.
• Fluoroquinolones, including levofloxacin, were emphasized to mitigate unfavorable outcomes and prevent acquired rifampicin resistance in isoniazid‑resistant TB, reinforcing updated treatment guidelines.
2025-09 · 900 articles · escalating

• Experts outlined the persistent hurdles in TB drug discovery, emphasizing the need for new therapeutic targets and better pipelines to combat the disease's high mortality and resistance potential.
• A study documented the transmission of bedaquiline‑resistant Mycobacterium tuberculosis in KwaZulu‑Natal, South Africa, warning that resistance to this key drug could undermine modern DR‑TB regimens.
• Researchers applied AI to field data, improving detection and prediction of bovine tuberculosis in Tunisian cattle, highlighting the zoonotic and economic stakes of TB beyond human patients.
2025-10 · 863 articles · escalating

• Indonesia adopted a COVID‑era integrated management model to accelerate its goal of eliminating tuberculosis by 2030, signaling a major policy shift in TB control.
• Researchers demonstrated that whole‑lung CT radiomics combined with clinical features can differentiate multidrug‑resistant TB from drug‑sensitive TB, offering a new tool for early detection of resistant strains.
• A nationwide Korean cohort study compared the safety of intradermal Danish‑1331 versus transcutaneous Tokyo‑172 BCG strains, finding comparable safety profiles and informing future vaccination strategies.

• Molecular diagnostics are gaining traction, with experts in New York noting incremental progress toward case reduction, while a novel phage‑based qPCR assay has enabled accurate detection of mycobacterial co‑infections in cattle, improving bovine TB surveillance.
• A case report of pulmonary TB in an immunocompromised patient raised questions about the interplay of HIV, COVID‑19, and TB, highlighting the heightened vulnerability of co‑infected individuals.

• The FDA‑approved vital staining method was shown to effectively monitor drug‑resistant pulmonary TB patients on extended regimens, offering a simple early‑failure detection tool under the NTEP.
• Researchers reported surface‑engineered ethambutol‑loaded nanoparticles that improve oral bioavailability and target delivery, addressing key limitations of current TB chemotherapy.
• The United States signed multi‑billion‑dollar five‑year health agreements with Nigeria ($5.1 b) and Kenya ($2.5 b), explicitly targeting infectious diseases including TB, to strengthen health systems and expand treatment capacity.

• The Indian Council of Medical Research reported that shorter TB treatment regimens are both clinically effective and cost‑effective, suggesting a potential shift in national treatment protocols.
• Malaysian lawmakers raised TB outbreaks as a key issue in a parliamentary session, indicating growing governmental concern alongside trade and labour topics.
• In Johor, three school pupils recovered from TB while four remain under monitoring, and a separate kidnapping case involved a mother receiving TB treatment, highlighting the disease's social impact.

• The World Health Organization issued an urgent call for sustained collective action to end TB, warning it remains a major public‑health threat especially in the Eastern Mediterranean region.

• A forensic case of disseminated TB with granulomatous pericarditis underscored the diagnostic challenges the disease poses in unexpected deaths.
• Research unveiled novel treatments—a photothermal‑silver nanoparticle nanoplatform and the antibiotic Questiomycin A targeting fabD—offering new hope against intracellular and multidrug‑resistant TB.

April 2026 saw AI-driven diagnostic breakthroughs in India, rising TB hotspots in North America, and a rare successful treatment of severe disseminated TB.

• Regional TB burdens remained high, with Saskatchewan identified as one of Canada’s provinces with the highest rates and Texas reporting 1,279 cases in 2024, prompting local health officials to intensify control efforts.
• A case of fulminant disseminated TB presenting as Landouzy sepsis was successfully managed with ECMO support and an individualized antituberculous regimen, highlighting the potential of advanced critical‑care strategies for rare, severe TB forms.
2026-05 · 546 articles · escalating
May 2026 saw new high‑dose rifampicin evidence, a rapid portable TB test, funding threats, and rising vaccine hesitancy in China.
• A systematic review and meta‑analysis found that daily regimens with high‑dose rifampicin may be more efficacious and safe than standard doses, which sit at the lower end of the exposure‑response curve.
• The Minidock MTB portable test delivers near‑point‑of‑care TB detection in minutes with about 86% sputum sensitivity, ~80% tongue‑swab sensitivity and >97% specificity, matching Xpert Ultra while enabling non‑invasive sampling.
• The U.S. administration is seeking to redirect $2 billion from global health programs to cover costs associated with the closure of USAID, threatening funding for TB control initiatives.
• In China, an increasing number of parents are refusing infant inoculations, including the tuberculosis BCG jab, intensifying a public‑health crisis as the government pushes to contain it.
