Nine ways to explore the research, run on this disease

Each method below is one or two MCP calls. The card gives the question it answers, the call exactly as made, what came back, a reading of that result and its limits.

Chaining them. Begin with faceted browsing to see the shape of the literature, then use the frontier scan to find recent work and adjacent topics. Next, test a specific claim with evidence triangulation, and use the head-to-head comparison to set it against a rival such as the RTS,S vaccine. Where a claim has several parts, such as rainfall and transmission, gap mapping shows which links have a literature, and cross-row screening should then be used to pull effect sizes, though here it returned none. Framing drift and the geography lanes give context on tone and on who is studying and reporting. Count history returned nothing and should be rerun before any statement about growth. Throughout, empty or off-topic results should be read as search limits, and key papers should be read directly.

Head-to-head claims

Question. Which of two interventions has more and stronger evidence?

How. compare_claims measures both claims the same way: papers on both poles, the corpus stance mix, the documents read and the strongest source.

{"tool": "compare_claims", "arguments": {"claim_a": "Insecticide-treated nets reduce malaria incidence", "claim_b": "The RTS,S vaccine reduces clinical malaria in children"}}

Verdict. the first claim has more evidence behind it

Reading. On the same measures, nets have 6,190 papers on both poles against 1,636 for the RTS,S vaccine, and the tool's verdict is that the first claim has more evidence behind it. Contested share is 22.8 for nets and 34.0 for the vaccine, so the vaccine literature is more disputed, with 528 qualifying papers against 518 supporting. Document reading is sparse on both sides: 1 supporting document each, none contradicting, and many unrelated (13 for nets, 11 for the vaccine, with 2 unread). The strongest source for nets is a 2013 review on use during pregnancy, while for the vaccine it is a 2025 randomised trial in Ghana and Kenya, a stronger design. Paper volume is therefore not the same as design strength, and the verdict reflects volume. The two claims also concern different tools, so a researcher should compare the trial-level effect measures directly instead of relying on counts.

The year in the news

From mid‑2025 the malaria community ramped up regional drug‑resistance surveillance and pledged elimination goals, while funding cuts sparked anxiety but also yielded a first‑in‑class pediatric treatment. Seasonal rains and climate‑driven vector expansion heightened transmission risk, prompting new global financing commitments and the rollout of the RTS,S vaccine in Cameroon. Despite continued mortality above 600,000 and emerging threats such as invasive Anopheles stephensi, several locales reported sharp prevalence declines, notably Zanzibar’s 0.04% rate and Lagos’ 2% rate, and India announced a 97% case reduction. The period closed with a mix of renewed donor support, novel therapeutic research, and policy actions aimed at sustaining gains amid persistent financing gaps.
